Elizabeth McKenna

Assistant Professor of Public Policy
John F. Kennedy School of Government, Harvard University

I am an Assistant Professor of Public Policy at the Harvard Kennedy School, an Andrew Carnegie Fellow (2025 to 2027), and director of the Civic Power Lab. I co-chair the Bloomberg Harvard Leading Civic Engagement in Cities program and am a member of the Organizing Forum.

Previously, I served as faculty principal investigator of the Democratic Self-Governance Project and worked for nine years with Marshall Ganz on the manuscript that became People, Power, Change (Oxford, 2024). I earned my Ph.D. in Sociology from the University of California, Berkeley, where my dissertation won the American Sociological Association's Best Dissertation Award.

I am a sociologist of democracy. I study how individuals become collective actors capable of shaping public life, and under what conditions that capacity strengthens or erodes democratic institutions. My research examines civic actors across the ideological spectrum, on the premise that democracy-building and democracy-corroding movements frequently share organizational forms. It runs in three streams: democracy and civil society in comparative perspective, anchored in Brazil; protest and organizing in American politics; and democratic agency, the capacity of ordinary people to act together on matters of public life.

I am currently finishing a book manuscript titled The Paradox of Participation: Organizing Authoritarianism in Democratic Brazil, under review at Cambridge University Press, which explains how the same civil society that helped democratize Brazil also helped upend it. My fourth book, The Regulars: The Making and Measure of Democratic Agency, asks how organizations turn ordinary people into committed volunteer leaders. I am also sole editor of Organized: The Mamdani Campaign and the Case for People-First Politics.

To produce research that informs practice, my lab built POLIS and Groundwork, the largest behavioral database of civic organizing yet assembled, under data use agreements with 38 movement organizations. My work has helped philanthropy measure civic capacity through Power Metrics and the Civic Power report, informed the Biden-Harris initiative on public participation in federal rulemaking, and been adopted as required reading for field organizers across the political spectrum.

I am the recipient of the Andrew Carnegie Fellowship (2024), the HKS Teaching Innovation Award (2026), the Analyst Institute's Expy Award for political research innovation (2026), the Michael Harrington Book Award from the American Political Science Association (2022), and the Best Dissertation Award from the American Sociological Association (2021), as well as a Dean's Teaching Award in every semester I have taught at HKS.
