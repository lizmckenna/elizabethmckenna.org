I am an Assistant Professor of Public Policy at the Harvard Kennedy School, an Andrew Carnegie Fellow (2025 to 2027), and director of the Civic Power Lab. I co-chair the Bloomberg Harvard Leading Civic Engagement in Cities program with Archon Fung. I earned my Ph.D. in Sociology from UC Berkeley in 2020.

My new book, The Paradox of Participation: Organizing Authoritarianism in Democratic Brazil (full manuscript available upon request, under review at Cambridge University Press for the Contentious Politics series), argues that civic participation strengthens democracy only when members govern the organizations they join and where those organizations' claims include all their fellow citizens, and that the same organizational capacities can otherwise build authoritarian power. My fourth book, The Regulars: The Making and Measure of Democratic Agency, asks how organizations turn ordinary people into committed volunteer leaders. Data collection is complete, one chapter is available as a working paper, and I've written about early findings in the Boston Globe here and here. I am also the editor of Organized: The Mamdani Campaign and the Case for People-First Politics, an edited volume with ten contributors on the 2025 Mamdani campaign for New York City mayor and the decade of organizing infrastructure that made it possible; manuscript delivery Spring 2027.

To produce research that informs practice, my lab built POLIS and Groundwork, the largest behavioral database of civic organizing yet assembled, under data use agreements with 38 movement organizations. My work has helped philanthropy measure civic capacity through Power Metrics and the Civic Power report, informed the Biden-Harris initiative on public participation in federal rulemaking, and been adopted as required reading for field organizers across the political spectrum.

I am the recipient of the HKS Teaching Innovation Award (2026), the Analyst Institute's Expy Award for political research innovation (2026), the Michael Harrington Book Award from the American Political Science Association (2022), and the Best Dissertation Award from the American Sociological Association (2021), as well as a Dean's Teaching Award in every semester I have taught at HKS.
